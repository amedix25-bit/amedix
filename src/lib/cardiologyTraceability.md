## 2. ICD-11 coding

- Catalogue: `src/lib/cardiologyIcd11.js` — read from ICD-11 MMS Chapter 11, release **2026-01**
  (`https://icd.who.int/browse/2026-01/mms/en`), retrieved 2026-10-30. Every code stored is copied from that
  tabulation; the app never generates, completes or guesses a code.
- New: `src/lib/cardiologyCodingGuidance.js` adds, per code:
  - a coding instruction (which entry codes the acute event, which is chronic, which two subcategories
    describe one event, which categories are coded in addition);
  - recorded exclusions where the tabulation makes the relation unambiguous (for example STEMI vs NSTEMI for
    one event, old vs acute infarction, essential hypertension vs the hypertensive organ-damage categories);
  - a postcoordination statement: ICD-11 MMS carries site, agent and temporal detail through
    postcoordination, and **no cluster is generated by the app** — the detail is recorded in the note's own
    structured fields.
- **NOT transcribed, and stated in the product rather than filled in:** WHO's own inclusion and exclusion
  wording and any postcoordinated cluster. Every catalogue entry tells the clinician to confirm them in the WHO
  browser. This is a deliberate limitation, not an omission: inventing that text would be a fabricated
  authority.
- Diagnosis status: Suspected · Provisional · Confirmed · Differential · Historical · Resolved · In remission.
  Meaning of each is defined in `DIAGNOSIS_STATUS_MEANING`; a confirmed, resolved or in-remission diagnosis
  requires a documented basis, and a code alone never confirms a diagnosis.
- New audit (`auditCodedDiagnoses`) reports, without ever altering an entry: one event coded as both STEMI and
  NSTEMI; a differential recorded as confirmed; a confirmed diagnosis without basis; an acute category recorded
  as historical; subsequent infarction without the index event; an unresolved entry carrying a code; a
  non-primary entry recorded alone; duplicate code/role pairs. Output appears in the note text under
  "Coding review prompts (advisory — the clinician's documented diagnosis stands)".

## 3. Medication safety

`src/lib/cardiologyMedicationSafety.js` + `CardiologyMedicationSafetyField.jsx`, added to the four clinical
notes (consultation, admission, progress, discharge):

- Reconciliation status, sources checked, allergy review, contraindications, interactions.
- Renal function documented and the renal dose review recorded (never inferred).
- Nine high-alert classes with a documented review state each: antiplatelet, anticoagulant, antihypertensive,
  antiarrhythmic, statin/lipid, heart-failure (four pillars), diuretic, antianginal, vasoactive/inotrope —
  each with its required considerations and monitoring parameters.
- Gaps listed are **documentation** gaps (issue recorded without the action, renal value without a renal
  review, class not reviewed). They never block saving and never assert that a medicine is wrong.
- No numeric dosing threshold is stored in the app; dosing follows the current product label, verified by the
  prescriber and pharmacist.

## 4. Time-critical alerts

`src/lib/cardiologyEscalation.js` + `CardiologyEscalationField.jsx`, added to the same four notes. Eight
triggers, each with the guideline that defines why it is time-critical:

suspected ACS (ESC 2023) · myocardial infarction (ACC/AHA 2025) · unstable tachy/bradyarrhythmia (ESC AF 2024) ·
cardiogenic shock (ESC HF 2021) · acute decompensated heart failure (ACC/AHA/HFSA 2022) · haemodynamic
instability (ESC ACS 2023) · dangerous ECG abnormality (ESC ACS 2023) · severe hypertension with suspected acute
target-organ damage (ESC hypertension 2024).

Recorded per episode: trigger, recognition time, who was informed, escalation time, treatment delivered before
and during escalation, monitoring, response/outcome, referral or transfer. Every rendering carries the
statement that documentation never precedes treatment and that alerts are advisory, not a substitute for
clinical judgement. Nothing is auto-selected from another field, and no alert blocks saving or treatment.

## 5. Finding during implementation (retained policy, not bypassed)

`src/lib/noteSectionPolicy.js` retires any field whose **label** matches `/escalat/i`, so the new block was
dropped on first build. The policy was left intact — it exists to stop duplicate "Escalation Criteria" fields
returning to every specialty. The cardiology block was renamed ("Time-critical presentation — who was
informed, times, treatment delivered and response"), keeping the clinical requirement without reopening the
retired pattern. Verified by test: the field is present in exactly the four intended notes and in no other.

## 7. Not verified — stated plainly

1. **No live end-to-end run with an authorized test account.** Conditionals, save, reopen, edit, sign,
   addendum, permission and audit-trail behaviour were exercised through the modules and the build, not
   through the running portal with a real session. This must be done before the specialty is called complete.
2. **WHO source verification is partial.** Category codes and display terms come from the release recorded in
   the catalogue; WHO inclusion/exclusion wording and postcoordination clusters were not transcribed and were
   not re-checked page by page in this change.
3. **No cardiology sign-off.** The medication-safety considerations, the eight escalation triggers, the coding
   audit rules and the "not verified in browser" labels need review and approval by the cardiology lead and by
   pharmacy before clinical reliance.
4. **No synthetic case walkthrough in the UI.** The synthetic cases were exercised at module level only
   (coding states, audit rules, medication gaps, escalation gaps, serialized output).
5. DAPT/PRECISE-DAPT thresholds inherited from the previous paragraph remain product-label dependent and are
   still awaiting pharmacy confirmation.
